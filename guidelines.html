American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) Guidelines

Management of Acne Vulgaris (2024)

AAD Good Clinical Practice Recommendations

Topical Therapy

  • Benzoyl peroxide and topical retinoids are strongly recommended as first-line topical treatments.
  • Use fixed-dose combinations of topical agents with multiple mechanisms of action.

Systemic Antibiotic Therapy

  • Limit the use of systemic antibiotics.
  • When prescribed, combine systemic antibiotics with benzoyl peroxide and other topical therapies.

Oral Isotretinoin

Reserve oral isotretinoin for patients with severe acne who:

  • Have not responded to standard oral and topical therapies.
  • Experience significant psychosocial burden.
  • Are at risk of or have developed scarring.

NG198 — Acne vulgaris: management · Published 2021; updated 2026

NICE guidelines: key treatment principles

01

Individualized treatment

Based on acne severity and patient preference, considering the advantages and disadvantages of treatment options.

02

Fixed combination therapy

Adapalene + benzoyl peroxide is a first-line option for any acne severity.

03

Antibiotic stewardship

Avoid topical or oral antibiotic monotherapy and topical + oral antibiotic combinations.

04

12-week review

Review treatment response at 12 weeks and adjust as appropriate.

05

Oral isotretinoin

Consider for severe acne resistant to adequate standard therapy with systemic antibiotics and topical therapy.

06

Maintenance after improvement

Consider a fixed-dose combination of topical adapalene + benzoyl peroxide as maintenance, particularly for patients with a history of frequent relapse.
